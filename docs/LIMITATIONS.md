# Limitations

1. **Who is observed.** Outcomes cover only graduates who enrolled at an Indiana public college the fall after graduation, about a third of each class. Graduates at private, out-of-state or later-entry colleges are not observed, and the share who enroll differs across schools, so a school's outcomes describe a selected group.
2. **School-level only.** Every quantity is a school average. Nothing here supports conclusions about individual students, and the model must not be applied to them (ecological fallacy).
3. **Grade 8 attribution.** Most high schools do not test grade 8, so their cohort's grade 8 math rate is the school corporation's rate across its middle schools. In districts with several high schools this assigns the same value to all of them. Junior/senior high schools use their own rate; the audit shows they are predicted differently.
4. **COVID gap.** No state test was given in spring 2020, so the class of 2024 uses its grade 7 ILEARN results (2019). Grade 7 and grade 8 rates are on different footing; values are standardized within cohort to limit this.
5. **SAT begins with the class of 2023.** The full model therefore rests on one cohort transition (2023 → 2024). The multi-cohort drift check uses a lever set without SAT.
6. **Assessment change.** ISTEP+ (classes 2021–2022) and ILEARN (2023–2024) measure different things on different scales; only within-cohort standardized values are comparable.
7. **CRDC timing and quality.** CRDC is collected every two years and reported by schools. Algebra I timing is observed only within the high school building; students who passed Algebra I in a separate middle school appear as an absence of high school passers, which `alg1_hs` captures only indirectly. The 2017–18 extract lacks usable Algebra II enrollment; `adv_share` is therefore limited to the classes of 2023–2024.
8. **Extraction quirk.** The browser script that produced the committed CRDC extract coerces blank grade-7/8 Algebra I values to 0 and can sum a "not applicable" code with a real count (e.g. −2 + 30 = 28). Only the unused `a1e78`/`a1p78` columns are affected. `code/01b_fetch_urban.py` fixes this for future rebuilds.
9. **Name-based linking.** CHE files before 2024 carry no school number; schools were linked to the 2024 file by exact name (95.6%, 97.6%, 98.3% of schools in the 2021, 2022, 2023 files). Renamed public schools may drop out of earlier cohorts.
10. **Suppression.** CHE suppresses small cells. Math remediation is reported for few schools and is rare (about 4% of enrollees), so it cannot be modeled; this is reported as a finding, not hidden.
11. **Level drift.** Statewide first-year credits rose between cohorts; a model trained on one cohort under-predicts the next by a common amount. The ranking of schools is unaffected, but absolute predictions need an annual intercept update.
12. **Associations, not effects.** Lever coefficients are cross-school associations conditional on the other levers. They do not show that adding calculus or expanding dual credit would raise a school's outcomes.
13. **Poverty.** Free/reduced-price lunch share alone predicts GPA about as well as the lever model. CCD lunch counts are affected by Community Eligibility Provision reporting, so `frl_share` is itself an imperfect poverty measure; it is used only for the audit and a sensitivity check.
14. **Directory vintage.** Public-school status and corporation membership come from the 2025–26 IDOE directory; schools that closed or changed corporation earlier may be misassigned or dropped.
